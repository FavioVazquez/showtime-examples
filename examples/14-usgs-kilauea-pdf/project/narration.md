## hook
In 2018, Kīlauea's summit collapsed as its magma drained away to the lower East Rift Zone.
<!-- K1 -->

## where
Kīlauea is on the Island of Hawaiʻi. This summary follows a two-page report by the U.S. Geological Survey.
<!-- K2 K3 -->

## start
On April 30, the Puʻu ʻŌʻō vent collapsed. Fissure eruptions began on May 3, and the next day a magnitude 6.9 earthquake struck.
<!-- K4 K5 K6 -->

## summit
Meanwhile, the summit sank. By mid-May, shaking and ground tilt had forced the Hawaiian Volcano Observatory out of its building. From May 29, the summit collapsed almost daily, each with the energy of a magnitude 5 earthquake.
<!-- K7 K8 K9 -->

## fissure8 {pause_after=1.1}
Down the rift, fissure 8's fountains rose 200 feet at times. [pause 0.9] Its river of lava reached the ocean at Kapoho Bay on June 3.
<!-- K10 K11 -->

## numbers
Lava covered 13.7 square miles. Hawaiʻi County counted 716 homes destroyed. Ocean entries built 875 acres of new land, and about 60,000 earthquakes shook the area.
<!-- K12 K13 K14 K15 K16 -->

## end
In early August, the eruption faded. On September 22, the national park partly reopened.
