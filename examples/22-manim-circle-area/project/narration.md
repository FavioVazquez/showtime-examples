## hook {pause_after=0.6}
Why is the area of a circle pi r squared?

## cutting {pause_after=0.5}
Cut the circle into thin rings, like an onion.

## ringlength {pause_after=0.7}
Each ring is about as long as its circumference, and very thin.

## unrolling {pause_after=0.6}
Straighten each ring into a strip, and stack them: shortest on top, longest at the bottom.

## pileup {pause_after=1.4}
They pile up into something close to a triangle.

## refining {pause_after=0.6}
Now make the rings thinner. Smaller steps.

## limit {pause_after=1.0}
As the rings get thinner, the steps vanish, and the stack becomes a triangle.

## heightline {pause_after=0.6}
Its height is the radius, r.

## baseline {pause_after=0.8}
Its base is the outermost ring, unrolled: the circumference, two pi r.

## halfbh {pause_after=0.6}
A triangle's area is half the base times the height.

## result {pause_after=0.9}
Half of two pi r, times r, is pi r squared.

## ending
Same area as the circle: pi r squared.
