Title: Why a Circle's Area Is πr² (a 70-second picture proof)
Description: Cut a circle into thin rings, like an onion. Straighten each ring into a strip and stack the strips: they pile up into something close to a triangle. As the rings get thinner the steps vanish, and in the limit the stack is a triangle whose height is the radius r and whose base is the circumference 2πr. Half the base times the height is πr², the same area as the circle.
This is one way to see why, stated as a limit argument; it is not the only proof.
